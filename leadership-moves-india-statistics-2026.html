Boardroom Moves announced 79 senior leadership moves in India between 6 December 2025 and 30 October 2026. 63 of the 79 leaders (80%) joined from another organisation and 15 (19%) were promoted inside their company or group. Sales and business development (13), HR and talent (12) and business head roles (12) moved most, and technology and IT services was the busiest sector (18 moves). These counts cover the announcements Boardroom CXO tracked, not every leadership move in India.

What these numbers cover

Every count on this page is a move that Boardroom Moves announced on the Boardroom CXO LinkedIn page: a senior appointment of a leader working in India, almost always at a company in India (a few roles also cover other regions), taken from the leader's own public announcement. That is a sample of the moves we tracked, not a census of leadership hiring in India. A sector or function with more moves here means we announced more of them, which may or may not match the whole market.

Function and sector groups are read from the job title, the company's business and what the post says the role does. A move counts as a promotion only when the post says the leader was elevated or already worked at the company or its group; it counts as an outside hire when the post shows the leader arriving from another employer. One leader, Naveen Kumar Rai, moved twice in the period, so the 79 moves cover 78 people.

Moves by city

Most announcements do not name a city: 74 of the 79 posts give none. Where a post does name the city of the role, it was Pune (2), Delhi (1), Hyderabad (1), Indore (1). That is too few to rank cities.

The wider market: senior hiring demand in India

For a view of the whole market rather than the moves we tracked, Naukri JobSpeak, October 2026 (Info Edge) measures new white-collar job postings on Naukri.com. It reported that hiring grew 14% year on year in October 2026, and that hiring for professionals with more than 16 years of experience grew 22%, led by auto and FMCG. It also showed gems and jewellery hiring up 21% year on year. These are job postings, not appointments, so they show demand for senior people rather than who moved.
