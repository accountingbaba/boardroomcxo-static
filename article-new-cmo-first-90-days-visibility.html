Your First 90 Days as a New CMO in Retail or D2C: the Visibility Part

October 2026 · 9 min read · Marketing Leadership · By CA Ketul Patel

Key Takeaways

  • Weeks 1 to 2: let the company announce first, then update your LinkedIn profile the same day. Profile edits show to visitors as soon as you save them.
  • Month 1: be seen inside the company before outside it. In many D2C companies marketing is founder-led, so the team needs to see your work before the market hears your views.
  • By day 90, a search of your name should show the new title and company. If it still shows the old job, fix that first.

In your first 90 days as a new CMO in retail or D2C, get visible in this order: the announcement and your LinkedIn profile in weeks 1 to 2, the people inside the company in month 1, and your own posts and the trade press in months 2 to 3. All the way through, make sure a search of your name shows the new role.

Marketing leaders move often. Boardroom Moves tracked 48 senior leadership moves in India between 17 and 29 October 2026, and marketing and brand roles were the largest group, 18 of the 48. These are the moves we tracked, not every move in India, and the details are in our October 2026 report. In the US, Spencer Stuart's January 2026 snapshot puts average CMO tenure in the S&P 500 at 4.1 years, against 5.0 years for all C-suite roles. Consumer companies had the shortest CMO tenure of the industries it analysed, at 3.5 years.

The same snapshot found that 73% of S&P 500 CMOs are first-time CMOs. In a role that often does not last long, the first 90 days carry real weight.

At Boardroom CXO we hire marketing leaders for retail, jewellery and D2C brands (see marketing leadership hiring), and through Boardroom Moves we record senior leadership moves in India every week. This guide covers only the visibility part of the first 90 days: what people see of you, inside and outside the company. It does not cover the marketing plan itself.

Before day one: agree your title and how the move is announced

Before you start, agree the exact title, the start date, one headshot and who announces first. Every later step depends on these being the same everywhere.

Marketing titles vary more than most. Spencer Stuart's 2025 study found that only 40% of Fortune 500 marketing leaders were in roles simply titled chief marketing officer. A title that reads one way in the company post and another way on your profile confuses anyone who searches for you later.

Pick one version of the title and use it in the company post, your LinkedIn profile and any press note. The full order for the announcement, including the stock exchange filing for listed companies, is in how to announce a new CXO appointment in India.

Weeks 1 to 2: let the announcement go out, then update LinkedIn

In the first two weeks, let the company announce first, then update your LinkedIn headline and current role the same day, and post a short note of your own.

Timing matters here. LinkedIn's help page says anyone who views your profile sees your edits as soon as you save them, even if you choose not to notify your network. So if you change your profile a week early, people can see the move before the company has said anything. Make the change on the day of the announcement.

LinkedIn also lets you choose whether to notify your network about a job change. For this one, turn it on. On the day, update these:

  • Headline: the agreed title and the company name, exactly as in the company post.
  • Current role: the title, the start date and two lines on what you lead.
  • About: the first two lines, which are all most people read, should mention the new role.
  • Photo: the same headshot the company used.
  • Featured: add the company announcement now, and any article about the move later.

Keep your own first post short. Thank the old team, say why you joined, tag the company and reshare the company post. Do not share plans or numbers yet. It is too early, and your founder or CEO may want to say them first.

Month 1: be visible inside the company before outside it

In month 1, put your time into being seen inside the company: stores, the warehouse, customer service, the sales team and the founder's office. Outside posting can wait.

This matters more in retail and D2C than in many sectors. At an MMA India board discussion, reported by the Free Press Journal in June 2025, members noted that in many unicorns and D2C companies marketing is founder-led and marketers are seen as optional. A new CMO who is loud on LinkedIn in week three, before the team has seen any work, can look like proof of that view.

Being visible inside looks like this:

  • Visit stores or fulfilment centres and talk to the staff who face customers.
  • Read customer service tickets and reviews, or sit in on calls.
  • Meet each function head one to one, including finance and supply chain.
  • At the end of month 1, send a short written note to the leadership team on what you heard.
  • When the team ships something, credit them by name inside the company.

Months 2 to 3: when to start posting, and on what

Start posting on LinkedIn from month 2, about once a week, on what you are learning about the customer and the category, not on results you do not have yet.

Topics that work for a new CMO in retail or D2C:

  • What you heard from store staff or customers, with no confidential numbers.
  • A campaign or launch the team shipped, with the credit going to them.
  • Roles you are hiring for in the marketing team.
  • One clear view on your category, based on something you saw.
  • A thank you to an agency or partner the team works with.

Topics to avoid: your old employer, numbers the company has not made public, criticism of what was done before you joined, and promises about the brand's future. If the founder is the public face of the brand, agree with them who says what. Your posts should support theirs, not compete with them.

Trade press in marketing: what gets a new CMO covered

For a new CMO, marketing trade press has two openings: the move itself, and later a real story, such as a campaign, a rebrand or a launch.

The move goes to the people-moves sections. afaqs! has a People Spotting section on appointments at agencies, brands and media companies, and Storyboard18 runs CXO Moves roundups in its Brand Makers section. Send the company announcement, the headshot and three lines on past roles to these desks on the day of the announcement. Send it once.

The second opening comes in months 2 to 3, or later. Trade journalists want a story: a campaign, a new brand position, a launch, or a view on the category backed by something you did. "A new CMO has big plans" is not a story. We cover how this works in how a new leader gets covered by Indian business media.

Make sure a search of your name shows the new role

By day 90, a Google search of your name should show the new title and company on the first page. If it shows the old job, fix that first.

Search your name in a private window once a month and look at what comes up: your LinkedIn profile, the company site, trade press and anything from your old employer. When we research a senior candidate, a Google search of their name is one of the first things we check, and boards, investors and future employers do the same. A 90-day visibility checklist

Here is the whole plan on one page, so you can check it against your own calendar in the first week of the new role.

  • Before day one: agree the title, start date, headshot and who announces first.
  • Weeks 1 to 2: company announces, you update LinkedIn the same day with notify on, you post a short thank you.
  • Month 1: stores, customers, function heads and finance. A written note to leadership at the end of the month.
  • Months 2 to 3: one post a week on what you are learning. A trade press pitch when there is a real story.
  • Day 90: search your name. The first page should show the new role.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a new CMO update their LinkedIn profile?

On the day the company announces the move, not before. LinkedIn shows your edits to anyone who views your profile as soon as you save them, even if you do not notify your network. Updating early can reveal the move before the company is ready. Update the headline, current role, About and photo together.

When should a new CMO start posting on LinkedIn?

From month 2, about once a week. Use month 1 to be seen inside the company: stores, customers, function heads and finance. In retail and D2C, where marketing is often founder-led, the team should see your work before the market hears your views. Post on what you are learning, not on results.

What should a new CMO post about in the first 90 days?

What you heard from customers and store staff, campaigns the team shipped with credit to them, roles you are hiring for, and one clear view on the category. Avoid your old employer, numbers the company has not made public, criticism of earlier work and promises about the brand's future.

How does a new CMO get covered by marketing trade press in India?

Send the company announcement, a headshot and three lines on past roles to people-moves sections such as afaqs! People Spotting and Storyboard18 CXO Moves on the day of the announcement. A second, bigger chance comes later, when there is a real story such as a campaign, a rebrand or a launch.
