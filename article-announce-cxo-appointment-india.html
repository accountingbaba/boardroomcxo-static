How to Announce a New CXO Appointment in India

October 2026 · 6 min read · Boardroom Moves · By CA Ketul Patel

Key Takeaways

  • Announce in this order: the stock exchange filing if the company is listed, then the company announcement, then the leader's own LinkedIn post, then the outlets that track moves.
  • Agree the exact title, start date and headshot before anyone posts. A title that reads differently in three places is a common mistake.
  • A LinkedIn post scrolls away in days. A page on an indexed website is what comes up when someone searches the leader's name months later.

To announce a new CXO appointment in India, go in this order: the stock exchange filing if the company is listed, then the company's own announcement, then the leader's LinkedIn post, and then the outlets that track leadership moves. Each step uses the same title, the same start date and the same photo.

Order matters because moves are frequent and easy to miss. Sapphire Human Capital tracked 4,361 CXO and senior leadership movements in India between April 2025 and March 2026, as reported by Business Today and MediaNews4U in August 2026. That is more than 80 a week. A single post on a busy week gets lost.

At Boardroom CXO we run executive search for retail, jewellery and D2C brands, and through Boardroom Moves we record senior leadership moves in India every week. This guide is what we see work, written for the new leader and for the founder or HR head announcing them.

Step 1: agree the facts before anyone posts

Before any announcement, the company and the leader should agree five things in writing: the exact title, the start date, the reporting line, a one-line description of the role and one approved headshot.

This sounds basic, but it is where many announcements go wrong. The company post says "Chief Marketing Officer", the leader's LinkedIn says "CMO, India", and a trade site writes "Head of Marketing". Anyone searching later sees three different jobs. Pick one version of the title and use it everywhere.

Also agree who goes first. In most cases the company announces and the leader reshares and posts the same day.

Step 2: if the company is listed, the stock exchange filing comes first

Listed companies in India must tell the stock exchanges about changes in directors, key managerial personnel and senior management under SEBI's Listing Regulations, so the filing comes before any public post.

These filings are made under Regulation 30 of the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, read with Para A of Part A of Schedule III. You can see real examples on the BSE and NSE websites. Nestlé India, for instance, filed one for senior management changes in December 2024. The format and the details to include are set by SEBI circulars.

The timing rules are strict and they change, so ask the company secretary when the filing will go out, and plan the LinkedIn posts and press note for after it. Private companies have no such filing, so they start at Step 3.

Step 3: the company announcement

The company announcement should be short: who has joined, in what role, from when, where they worked before, and one line on what they will lead. It goes on the company's LinkedIn page and, if there is one, its website newsroom.

A quote from the CEO or founder helps, but only a real one that they have approved. Add the approved headshot. If the company has a press or "in the news" page, add the announcement there too, so it has a permanent home on the company's own site.

Step 4: the leader's own LinkedIn post

The leader's LinkedIn post should go up the same day as the company post, after the profile headline and current role are updated to match the agreed title.

Keep it simple: thank the previous team, say what drew you to the new company, and tag the company. Reshare the company's post as well. Update the headline first, because people who see the post will click through to the profile, and a profile that still shows the old job causes confusion.

Where leadership moves in India get covered

Leadership moves in India are covered by trade publications for each sector and by sites and LinkedIn pages that track appointments. These are the ones we see most often, as of October 2026:

  • Storyboard18 runs regular "CXO Moves" roundups in its Brand Makers section, mostly marketing, media and consumer brand moves.
  • People Matters reports leadership appointments for an HR audience and has run a weekly tracker called Movement Matters.
  • The Economic Times industry sites, such as ETRetail and ETHRWorld, report appointments in their sectors.
  • IndianRetailer.com reports appointments at retail and consumer companies.
  • CXO Movements is a site that tracks executive leadership changes across industries, gathered from public news.
  • Boardroom Moves by Boardroom CXO posts a weekly Brief on LinkedIn every Thursday with 5 to 6 leaders who moved that week, written from public announcements, with nothing asked of the leader. Moves are also listed on boardroomcxo.com/moves, and each month we publish the counts, as in the October 2026 report.

The trade outlets choose what they cover. To give a move a better chance, send the company announcement, the headshot and three lines on previous roles to the journalist who writes the appointments column. Send it once, on the day of the announcement.

Make sure the move leaves a page that ranks for your name

The part of an announcement that lasts is the page that comes up when someone searches the leader's name, so it is worth making sure one exists.

LinkedIn posts drop out of the feed within days. Months later, a board member, an investor or a future employer will search your name, and they will see whatever pages Google shows. Common mistakes to avoid

Most weak announcements come from a handful of avoidable mistakes:

  • A different title in the company post, the LinkedIn profile and the press.
  • For listed companies, posting on LinkedIn before the stock exchange filing.
  • No headshot, so outlets either skip the story or use an old photo.
  • Changing the LinkedIn headline days after the post, so early visitors see the old role.
  • Leaving the announcement only on LinkedIn, with no page on the company site or anywhere else that will still be found next year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a listed company have to disclose a new CXO appointment?

Yes. Under Regulation 30 of the SEBI Listing Regulations, read with Para A of Part A of Schedule III, listed companies disclose changes in directors, key managerial personnel and senior management to the stock exchanges. Check the exact timeline and format with the company secretary.

Should the company or the leader announce first?

The company first. If it is listed, the stock exchange filing goes out before anything else. Then the company post, then the leader updates their LinkedIn profile and posts the same day.

Where are leadership moves in India reported?

Trade publications such as Storyboard18 (CXO Moves), People Matters, ETRetail, ETHRWorld and IndianRetailer.com, tracking sites such as CXO Movements, and LinkedIn series such as Boardroom Moves by Boardroom CXO.

Do I need a press release for a CXO appointment?

Not always. A listed company needs its stock exchange filing. For a private company, a short announcement on the company LinkedIn page and website is often enough, sent to the trade journalists who cover appointments in that sector.
